01The founder

Ploenpit Rianmek — known to everyone as Pa Pim.

Every weekend, her eldest son used to take her to specialty cafés. After three decades of brewing for her family at the kitchen counter, she retired — and refused, in her words, to “sit around or nap, wasting oxygen.”

In February 2019 she set a British-designed ROK manual espresso maker on a two-stool wooden stand near Hua Lamphong. Within weeks the photos were everywhere. That November she moved upstairs of a working mechanic’s shophouse on a Talat Noi alley. The ground floor is still scrap metal and engine parts. The second floor — once you find the staircase — is the most quietly beloved coffee bar in old Bangkok.

“My life is already sweet, so I don’t see the need to add sugar. Doesn’t matter if you want it not sweet or super sweet, ‘cause I got no sugar to give.” — Pa Pim · Khaosod English, 2019

She doesn’t brew every cup anymore — her son does most of the pulling now, and he’s the one who paints the artwork on the walls. But Pa Pim still rotates between branches by mood. “I don’t even know which shop I’ll be at,” she’s said. “It all depends on my mood.”

1 The original stand was on Mahaphruttharam Road. The ROK is a manual lever espresso maker — no electricity, all forearm. She still uses it for the signature Iced Latte On The ROK. Sources: Khaosod English (May 2019), Coconuts Bangkok (Nov 2019), BK Magazine (Feb 2020), Malay Mail (Feb 2022).
